Emergency Preparation

Emergency situations

Start with the first action for your situation. Follow local emergency instructions as they are issued.

Call 911 if someone needs immediate police, fire, or medical help in the U.S.

Home fire

First action Get out, stay out, and call 911 from outside.

  1. Use your nearest safe exit. If there is smoke, get low and move under it. If a door feels warm or smoke is coming through, use another exit.
  2. Go to your outside meeting place. Tell firefighters immediately if anyone or any pets may still be inside.
  3. If you can’t escape, close the door between you and the fire. Call 911 and tell the dispatcher where you are. Signal from a window if you can.
U.S. Fire Administration fire escape guidance

Flash flood or rising water

First action Move to higher ground immediately if you can get there safely.

  1. Keep out of floodwater on foot and in a vehicle. Turn around at a flooded road or barricade.
  2. If trapped by rising water in a building, move to the highest level. Avoid a closed attic where you could become trapped. Call 911 if you need rescue.
  3. Return only when local officials say it’s safe. Stay clear of damaged electrical equipment and downed lines.
Red Cross flood guidance

Tornado warning

First action Get to a basement, storm shelter, or a small windowless room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building.

  1. Stay away from windows. Crouch low and cover your head and neck. Use a mattress or heavy blanket for extra protection if one is within reach.
  2. Leave a mobile home for a nearby sturdy building or designated shelter if you can reach it safely. Keep away from highway overpasses if driving.
  3. Keep monitoring official updates from shelter until the warning has ended. Watch for damaged structures, debris and downed lines afterward.
National Weather Service tornado guidance

Power outage

First action Check anyone who relies on powered medical equipment. Use backup power or move to a powered location if safe. Call 911 for an immediate medical emergency.

  1. Use flashlights, keep your refrigerator and freezer doors closed, and report the outage to your utility. Stay away from downed power lines.
  2. Run portable generators outdoors, more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Keep grills and camp stoves outdoors too.
  3. Discard refrigerated perishables after four hours without power unless they were kept at 40°F (4°C) or colder. Seek a safe place with heating or cooling if the temperature at home becomes unsafe.
Red Cross outage guidance CDC generator safety Federal food safety chart

Evacuation order

First action Leave promptly using the route local officials recommend. If it’s unsafe, follow official alternate directions.

  1. Take people and pets. Grab medications, mobility aids, keys, and your go-bag if they’re within reach and doing so won’t delay departure.
  2. Check local notices for shelter locations and transportation help. Once safe, tell your emergency contact where you are.
  3. Wait for officials to confirm it is safe before going home.
FEMA evacuation guidance Red Cross shelter finder

Unsafe tap water

First action Use commercially bottled water for drinking and cooking. Check which advisory your water utility or health department has issued.

  1. For a boil water advisory, use bottled water or boil tap water as instructed for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth and making ice. Follow the advisory for infant formula.
  2. For a do not drink advisory, use bottled water for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth. Boiling can’t make chemically contaminated water safe.
  3. For a do not use advisory, avoid using the tap water for any purpose. Follow local instructions until the advisory is lifted.
CDC guide to drinking water advisories

Shelter-in-place order

First action Follow the alert’s shelter instructions. The safest place depends on the hazard.

  1. If the alert is for a chemical release outdoors, get indoors. Close windows and doors, and turn off fans and systems that bring in outside air. Follow any instructions to seal the room.
  2. Keep your phone or a battery-powered radio available for updates. Follow the next instruction from local officials, including any change to evacuation.
  3. For a home fire, use the home fire steps. For a tornado warning, use the tornado shelter steps.
CDC shelter guidance for a chemical emergency
