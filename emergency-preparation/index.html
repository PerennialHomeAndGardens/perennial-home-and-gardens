Emergency Preparation

What to do
right now.

An emergency can happen before you have a plan. Start with the steps that protect you and the people around you.

Your first moves

  1. Protect yourself now.

    Leave a burning building. Go to higher ground in a flash flood. During a tornado warning, get to a basement or a small interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building.

  2. Check official instructions as soon as you can.

    Follow emergency alerts and local officials. If ordered to evacuate, leave right away by a safe route. If told to shelter, follow the instructions for that hazard.

  3. Once safe, check in.

    Account for everyone with you. Text your household or emergency contact where you are and whether you need help.

After you’re safe, make a plan.

Choose a meeting place near home and another outside your neighborhood. Pick a contact outside your area and share the plan with everyone in your household.

Plan how to move or shelter with children, pets, and anyone who needs mobility assistance, medication, or powered medical equipment.

Start with a three-day supply of food and water for evacuation. For home, the Red Cross recommends building toward two weeks. Budget one gallon of water per person per day and extra for pets.

Official alerts and shelter info

Look for weather watches and warnings, evacuation or shelter-in-place orders, drinking-water advisories, and notices about aid or supply distribution.

For weather alerts, a watch means get ready and a warning means act. Read the full alert for the area, the time, and the action to take. Check your water utility or health department for tap-water notices.
